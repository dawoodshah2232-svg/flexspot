> **Key takeaways:**
> - Most visibility gaps aren't exotic — they're missing listings, dead profiles, and unclaimed free placements.
> - Run this audit quarterly: score each area, fix the lowest scores first, and re-check in 90 days.
> - You don't need to be everywhere; you need to be findable and credible everywhere your customers actually look.
> - Document your baseline — the audit's real value is showing progress over time, not achieving perfection today.

## Why audit before you act

Most small businesses promote randomly: a few social posts here, an ad there, a directory listing from two years ago with the wrong phone number. A visibility audit replaces randomness with a map — it shows you, area by area, where your brand shows up well, where it's invisible, and where it's actively embarrassing you (outdated info is worse than no info).

Work through the checklist below honestly. Score each item as ✅ done, ⚠️ partial, or ❌ missing. Your lowest-scoring sections are your next 90 days of work — which pairs naturally with a [90-day online visibility plan](/blog/increase-online-visibility-small-business-90-days).

## Section 1: Search visibility (can people find you?)

1. ☐ Your site appears when searching your exact brand name.
2. ☐ Your Google Business Profile exists, is claimed, and has accurate hours, address, and phone.
3. ☐ Your site has a descriptive title tag and meta description on every important page (not "Home" or "Untitled").
4. ☐ Your site loads in a reasonable time on a mobile connection.
5. ☐ Your site is usable on a phone — readable text, tappable buttons, no horizontal scrolling.
6. ☐ You rank for at least a few specific, low-competition phrases related to what you sell (check in a private window).
7. ☐ Your business appears correctly on major maps and navigation apps.
8. ☐ You have at least a few pages that answer real customer questions (not just service descriptions).

## Section 2: Listings and directories (are you listed where buyers look?)

9. ☐ Your name, address, and phone number are consistent across every listing (inconsistency confuses search engines and customers).
10. ☐ You're listed in the 3–5 directories your industry actually uses (not 50 random ones).
11. ☐ Your listings have photos — real ones, not stock.
12. ☐ Your listings have at least a few genuine reviews, and you've responded to them.
13. ☐ Old, duplicate, or wrong listings have been claimed or removed.
14. ☐ Your WhatsApp / contact links work from every listing (test them).

## Section 3: Social presence (do you look alive?)

15. ☐ You have profiles on the 1–2 platforms where your customers actually are (not all six).
16. ☐ Profile bios clearly say what you do and link to your site.
17. ☐ Your most recent post is from this month — not last year (a dead profile is worse than none).
18. ☐ Pinned or featured content shows your best work or offer.
19. ☐ You respond to comments and messages within a reasonable time.
20. ☐ Your visual branding (logo, colors, photo style) is consistent across profiles.

## Section 4: Website and content (does it convert attention?)

21. ☐ A first-time visitor understands what you offer within seconds of landing.
22. ☐ Every key page has one clear call to action.
23. ☐ You show proof: reviews, testimonials, real photos, client names.
24. ☐ Pricing (or clear pricing ranges) is findable without contacting you.
25. ☐ Your contact page has a real phone number, email, and response-time expectation.
26. ☐ You have a way to capture visitors who aren't ready to buy (email signup, WhatsApp opt-in).

## Section 5: Reputation and press (do others vouch for you?)

27. ☐ You have a system for asking happy customers for reviews (not just hoping).
28. ☐ You've responded professionally to every negative review.
29. ☐ Your brand has been mentioned or featured somewhere you didn't pay for (press, blog, podcast, community) — or you have a plan to pursue it ([how to get featured in local media](/blog/how-to-get-your-small-business-featured-in-local-media)).
30. ☐ You monitor mentions of your brand name (free alerts set up).
31. ☐ You have at least one [featured placement or sponsorship](/blog/homepage-sponsorships) putting your brand in front of new audiences.

## Section 6: Offline and local (for businesses with a physical footprint)

32. ☐ Your signage is visible, clean, and matches your online branding.
33. ☐ You appear in local "best of" lists, community groups, or neighborhood guides.
34. ☐ You have a presence at local events, markets, or partnerships — or a plan for one.
35. ☐ Your packaging, receipts, or bags carry your brand and a way to find you online.
36. ☐ Staff know the one-line description of what you do and can say it naturally.

## Section 7: Measurement (do you know what's working?)

37. ☐ Analytics is installed and you check it at least monthly.
38. ☐ You track where new customers come from (ask them — see [visibility metrics](/blog/how-to-measure-brand-visibility)).
39. ☐ You know your branded search trend (are more people searching your name?).
40. ☐ You have a dated record of this audit to compare against next quarter.

## Scoring and next steps

Count your ✅ marks:

- **32–40:** Strong foundation. Focus on the ⚠️ partials and on deepening one or two channels.
- **20–31:** Typical small business. Your ❌ items are a clear, prioritized to-do list — start with Sections 1 and 2 (search and listings), which are mostly free fixes.
- **Under 20:** You have major visibility gaps, which is actually good news — it means large, cheap wins are available. Work the checklist top to bottom.

The audit is not a one-time event. Visibility decays: listings go stale, profiles die, competitors outrank you. Re-run it quarterly, keep the old scorecards, and watch the trend. A brand that audits and fixes steadily for a year looks nothing like one that promoted randomly for a year — and your [visibility metrics](/blog/how-to-measure-brand-visibility) will show it.

## How to run the audit in one afternoon

Block three hours and work section by section:

- **Hour 1:** Sections 1–2 (search + listings). Mostly checking and fixing: claim profiles, correct hours and phone numbers, add photos. These are quick wins — do them first for momentum.
- **Hour 2:** Sections 3–4 (social + website). Update bios, pin fresh content, fix the obvious website issues (headline, CTA, contact details). Note bigger website work for later; don't redesign today.
- **Hour 3:** Sections 5–7 (reputation, offline, measurement). Set up brand alerts, install or verify analytics, record your baseline scores in a dated document.

End the afternoon with two artifacts: your scored checklist and a prioritized fix-list of the ❌ items, ordered by effort-to-impact. Start executing the top five this week.

## Turning gaps into a 90-day plan

Group your ❌ items by theme and sequence them:

- **Days 1–30: Foundation fixes.** Listings, profiles, site basics, analytics, review-asking system. Mostly free, mostly one-time.
- **Days 31–60: Visibility building.** Start the one consistent activity your audit says you're missing — content, community presence, outreach for features, or a small placement test.
- **Days 61–90: Amplification.** Take what's showing early movement and invest more: the directory driving calls, the content format getting shared, the community responding to you.

Re-run the full audit on day 90. Compare scorecards. The sections that improved validate your effort; the ones that didn't tell you where the plan needs adjusting. Then run the next 90 days. Visibility is a practice, not a project.


File it where you'll find it in 90 days. The scorecard's power is comparative — a single audit is a snapshot; four quarterly scorecards are a story of compounding progress. That story, backed by your [metrics dashboard](/blog/how-to-measure-brand-visibility), is also the clearest possible answer to "is our marketing working?"
