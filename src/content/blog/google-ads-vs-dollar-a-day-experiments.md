It's the heavyweight vs. the scrappy underdog: Google Ads, the $200-billion giant, against a $1-a-day experiment on a public leaderboard. Which one should a startup with $100/month actually choose? Let's score them on the only metrics that matter at this budget.

## Round 1: What $30 buys

- **Google Ads:** At $2–$4 per click, $30 buys 8–15 clicks. If your site converts at 2% (generous for a new startup), that's 0.3 customers. You also spent 3 hours setting up campaigns, keywords, and negative lists.
- **$1-a-day leaderboard:** $30 buys 30 days of a live, public brand position + a referral link paying 20% instant commission on every referred payment + 4–5 visible rank jumps you can screenshot and share. Setup time: 10 minutes.

**Winner: $1-a-day.** It's not close at this budget.

## Round 2: Compounding

- **Google Ads:** Every dollar is spent once and gone. Stop paying, traffic stops instantly. Nothing compounds except Google's knowledge of your wallet.
- **$1-a-day:** Your referral link keeps earning per visit. Your rank history becomes social proof. Your screenshots become content. The leaderboard position keeps working between boosts.

**Winner: $1-a-day.** Paid clicks are rented; positions and referral networks are assets.

## Round 3: Learning

- **Google Ads:** You learn which keywords cost money. Valuable eventually, expensive now.
- **$1-a-day:** You learn which taglines get clicks, which communities send traffic, and what rank movement does to your visits — with [honest measurement](/blog/measuring-roi-of-one-dollar-ad-experiments) for $30 total.

**Winner: $1-a-day** for early-stage learning per dollar.

## When Google Ads wins

Honesty matters: Google Ads wins **later**. Once you have a converting funnel, proven messaging, and $500+/month, search intent becomes the highest-ROI channel in existence. The mistake isn't using Google Ads — it's using it *first*, before you can afford to learn.

## The verdict for startups

**Start with $1-a-day experiments.** Spend your first $100 learning what message and audience work ([here's how to split it](/blog/your-first-100-dollars-in-advertising)). Graduate to Google Ads when you have a funnel that converts and a budget that survives the learning phase.

The underdog doesn't win by fighting the giant's fight. It wins by picking a fight it can afford — and this one, it wins.
