Most ranking systems are black boxes. A search engine shuffles results with an algorithm nobody can see, and a social feed decides who gets attention behind closed doors. SpotFlex takes the opposite approach: the leaderboard is a public scoreboard, the scoring rule is one sentence long, and every move happens where everyone can watch it.

This guide explains exactly how rankings work, what moves a spot up or down, and how to read the board like a pro — whether you're holding a spot, backing one you love, or just exploring.

## The one rule that runs the board

SpotFlex is a public spotlight competition. Brands, creators, startups, and communities claim public spots, and those spots compete for attention on a live leaderboard.

The ranking rule is simple and transparent: **spots are ordered by total verified boosts.** A boost is a paid contribution that adds to a spot's total. Every dollar of verified buzz moves the spot up the board. When your spot passes another spot, you take its rank — live, in front of everyone.

There is no hidden engagement score, no mystery multiplier, no editorial thumb on the scale. If two spots disagree about who deserves to be on top, the leaderboard settles it with math anyone can audit: which total is bigger?

This matters for a reason most platforms won't admit. On ad networks and social feeds, your money buys *impressions* — an opaque number in a dashboard. On SpotFlex, your contribution buys *position* — a visible rank that everyone browsing the board can see. Position is the product.

## How a spot gets onto the board

Every spot starts with a claim. Claiming takes under a minute and requires no account: you enter your spot's details, choose a boost starting at $1, and complete payment. SpotFlex is currently in Phase 1, which means payments are completed manually and the team verifies them — usually within a few hours. Once verified, the boost counts toward the spot's total and the spot appears on the board.

Founding spots (numbers 1–100) can currently be claimed free while they're unclaimed, which means a founding brand can take its numbered spot onto the board without an initial spend — though it starts at the bottom of the table until boosts arrive.

Once live, a spot has a public profile page showing its rank, total boosts, views, clicks, and 7-day momentum. That profile is the spot's storefront on the platform: anyone can visit it, anyone can boost it, and anyone can share it.

## How boosts move a spot up

Boosting is the engine of the leaderboard. The creator of a spot can boost their own spot at any time, and — this is the part that makes the board interesting — **anyone else can boost any spot too.**

That means ranking isn't just a function of what a brand spends. A spot can climb because:

- **Its owner keeps boosting it** — the straightforward path. Top up your total and watch the rank rise.
- **Fans and customers boost it** — anyone can contribute to a spot they love, pushing it higher. This is effectively a public vote with money, and it's the fastest way to help a friend or a brand you admire reach #1.
- **It goes quiet and slides** — because ranks are relative, a spot that stops accumulating boosts can be overtaken by spots that keep moving. Standing still on a live board is the same as drifting backward.

The "live" part deserves emphasis. When you pass someone, you take their rank immediately — there's no batching, no daily recalculation. That instant feedback is what makes the board feel like a competition rather than a directory. Watching a spot climb in real time, overtaking rivals one by one, is the core spectacle of the platform.

One practical note: during Phase 1, boosts are verified manually by the team before they count. So a boost you submit may take a few hours to register on the board. Plan timed pushes with that in mind — if you're coordinating a launch-day surge, get your boosts in early.

## The compare tool: reading the board properly

A leaderboard tells you *who* is winning. It doesn't tell you *why*. That's what the compare tool is for.

Open any spot profile and hit "Compare," or go straight to flexspot.lol/compare. You can pick up to 3 spots and see them side by side on:

- **Rank** — current leaderboard position
- **Total boosts** — the verified total driving the rank
- **Views** — profile traffic
- **Clicks** — outbound clicks to the brand
- **Click-through rate** — clicks relative to views, a rough signal of how compelling the spot's pitch is
- **7-day momentum** — how the spot's trend looks over the last week

This is the closest thing the platform has to a strategy dashboard, and it's worth using before you spend. Compare your spot against the ones directly above you: is the gap in total boosts small enough to close with one push? Is a rival getting far more views but fewer clicks — suggesting their profile converts poorly, and your better pitch could win even on a smaller budget?

Momentum is the most underrated column. A spot with rising 7-day momentum is being actively backed; a spot with flat momentum is coasting on an old total and is ripe to be overtaken. When you're deciding where to put your next dollar, momentum tells you more than rank does.

## The side boards: momentum beyond raw spend

The main leaderboard is a pure boost-total race. But SpotFlex also runs side boards that surface different kinds of activity:

- **Most Shared** — the spots with the most referral clicks this week. This rewards distribution: whoever's link is traveling furthest across social apps and group chats.
- **Community Favorite** — the most profile views in the last 7 days. Pure attention, no spend required. A well-crafted spot profile can win this on merit alone.
- **Top Referrers** — the people driving the most referrals through their personal links. Every spot has a shareable referral link (flexspot.lol/s/your-brand?ref=YOUR-CODE), and referrers earn 20% commission on payments from people who join through their link. The people at the top of this board are the platform's distribution engine.

These boards matter because they create multiple ways to win. If you can't outspend the top of the main board, you can out-share them (Most Shared), out-pitch them (Community Favorite), or recruit the army that does the sharing for you (Top Referrers). Smart players treat the boards as a portfolio: a little spend on boosts, a lot of effort on distribution.

## What this means for spot holders

If you're holding a spot — or thinking about claiming one — the ranking system implies a simple playbook:

1. **Get on the board.** Claim your spot (from $1) so you exist in the competition. An unclaimed or unboosted spot is invisible; presence is the prerequisite.
2. **Study the board above you.** Use the compare tool to find the cheapest rank gains — the spots just ahead of you with small boost gaps and flat momentum.
3. **Boost in pushes, not drips.** Because overtaking is visible and dramatic, concentrated pushes create stories people notice and share. Ten dollars all at once is more valuable than ten dollars spread across ten days.
4. **Activate your fans.** Anyone can boost your spot. Give your audience a reason to — a milestone ("help us hit #5"), a rivalry ("overtake our competitor"), or just the spectacle of watching the rank climb live.
5. **Distribute through referrals.** Share your spot with your referral link so every new visitor and payment compounds: more visibility for the spot, 20% commission for you on payments they make.
6. **Watch the side boards.** If the main leaderboard is expensive territory, make a run at Most Shared or Community Favorite — they convert attention into credibility, and credibility converts into boosts.

## What this means for everyone else

You don't need a spot to enjoy the board. Browse the leaderboard, compare the contenders, and boost the brands you genuinely want to see win — it's the most direct vote of confidence the internet offers a small brand. A dollar on a spot you love does more than a like on a post: it moves a real number on a public board.

Rankings here aren't a mystery to decode. They're a contest to join. The rule fits in one sentence, the scoreboard is public, and the next move is always yours.
