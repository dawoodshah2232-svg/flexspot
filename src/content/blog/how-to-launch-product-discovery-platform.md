> **Key takeaways:**
> - A discovery-platform launch is a concentrated burst of attention — preparation matters more than the launch day itself.
> - Your listing needs a clear one-line promise, honest screenshots, and a specific ask (try it, vote, give feedback).
> - Launch on a day you can be fully present to answer questions and respond to every comment.
> - Day-one traffic is the start, not the finish: capture emails and feedback so the momentum compounds.
> - Don't buy fake votes or engagement — platforms and communities spot it instantly, and it destroys trust.

## Why discovery platforms are worth your launch

Product discovery platforms exist for one reason: concentrated attention from people who actively want to find new things. Unlike posting into the void of social media, a launch on a discovery board puts your product in front of browsers who arrived with the intent to explore. That intent is the whole game — a hundred curious visitors beat ten thousand scrolling thumbs.

But attention is only useful if you're ready for it. Here's how to be.

## Before launch: prepare the listing

### Nail the one-liner

You get one sentence to earn a click. It should say what the product does and who it's for — not how clever your branding is. "Invoice software for freelancers" beats "Reimagining financial workflows for the modern solopreneur" every time. Write ten versions, pick the clearest.

### Show, don't describe

Screenshots and short videos outperform paragraphs. Show the product doing the thing it promises: the dashboard, the result, the before-and-after. If your product is visual, lead with visuals. If it isn't, a 30-second screen recording of the core workflow works wonders.

### Prepare your ask

Every listing needs a call to action. "Try the free demo and tell us what breaks" is better than "check it out." Specific asks get specific responses — and responses are what the platform's ranking algorithms notice.

### Line up your first supporters

You don't need an army, but you need a few real people who'll show up on day one: beta users, friends in the industry, early customers. Message them personally the day before. Genuine early engagement signals to the platform that your launch is alive.

## Launch day: be present

### Answer everything

Every comment and question on launch day deserves a reply — fast. Founders who camp on their launch thread all day consistently outperform those who post and disappear. Questions are buying signals in disguise; treat them that way.

### Share the story, not just the link

When you post about the launch elsewhere, lead with the story: the problem you hit, what you built, what surprised you. "We launched today" with a bare link gets scrolled past. A two-paragraph story about why this exists gets clicked.

### Watch, don't just broadcast

Pay attention to which questions repeat — that's your onboarding gap. Notice which feature people ask about first — that's your real headline. Launch day is free user research; take notes.

## After launch: convert the spike into a slope

The launch spike fades. What remains depends on what you captured:

- **Email list:** Every visitor who doesn't convert to a user should have had a chance to subscribe for updates. A launch without an email capture is a launch you have to repeat from zero.
- **Feedback log:** Every piece of feedback, organized by theme. This becomes your next month's roadmap.
- **Testimonials:** Ask your happiest day-one users for a quote while the excitement is fresh. You'll use these everywhere.
- **Content:** Turn the launch story into a blog post, a thread, a short video. The launch is an event; the content about it works for months.

## What not to do

- **Don't buy votes or fake engagement.** Discovery communities are small and observant. Getting caught — and you will get caught — burns the exact audience you wanted to impress.
- **Don't launch half-broken.** "It's in beta" covers rough edges, not a signup flow that doesn't work. Test the critical path on a fresh browser before you go live.
- **Don't launch and vanish.** The founders who treat launch day as the finish line get launch-day results. The ones who treat it as the starting gun get companies.

A discovery-platform launch is one of the cheapest high-leverage moves an early product can make. Prepare the listing like it matters — because for 24 hours, it's the only thing about your product most people will ever see.
