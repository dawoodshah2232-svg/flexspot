> - The money is moving from linear TV to streaming, which means the audience your brand wants is already on the big screen — it's the buying that changed, not the viewer.

## The channel nobody told small brands about

For decades, television advertising had a bouncer at the door. Six-figure minimums, media agencies, production companies, national reach you didn't need at prices you couldn't afford. Social and search became the small-brand refuge because TV wouldn't let you in.

That door has opened — quietly, over the last two years. Roku launched a self-serve Ads Manager aimed squarely at growth and performance marketers. Amazon turned its Prime Video ad inventory into a self-serve product inside the same console brands already use for search ads. YouTube's TV-screen inventory has been buyable through Google Ads all along. The channel that used to demand a national brand now takes campaigns a local plumbing company could fund.

The money is already there. The Interactive Advertising Bureau projects global digital video ad spend rising 11% to roughly $82 billion in 2026, with connected TV taking about $29 billion of it — and more than half of incremental CTV budgets shifting straight out of linear television. eMarketer's US forecast puts streaming TV at around $38 billion this year, growing roughly 14%, with the crossover year — when streaming passes traditional TV — projected for 2028. Your audience has been moving to the big screen for years. This is the year the small-brand buying caught up.

## What "self-serve streaming" actually means

Three doors matter, and they work differently.

**Roku Ads Manager** is the friendliest entry point. Roku is both a streaming device platform (about one in five US TV viewing hours flows through its devices) and a top-five streaming service in its own right, and it surpassed 100 million streaming households worldwide this year. Its self-serve tool buys across Roku inventory plus roughly a hundred ad-supported streamers, with reported minimums around $500 per campaign and CPMs in the $25–35 band. The catch is creative: you upload a finished 15- or 30-second spot, so you need the video before you start.

**Amazon Sponsored TV** is the console route. Built inside Amazon Ads, it carries no minimum campaign spend, no daily minimum, and no upfront commitment. It needs Brand Registry enrollment, and you don't have to sell a single product on Amazon to use it — Amazon opened streaming TV to non-endemic brands, and inventory runs across Prime Video, Fire TV, and Twitch. The premium route through Amazon DSP still carries a recommended $10,000 campaign minimum, but the self-serve console is the small-brand door.

**YouTube via Google Ads** is the one you've had all along. Google Ads has no minimum spend, video campaigns run on TV screens through YouTube's CTV inventory, and the targeting and measurement machinery is the same one you already use for search and display. It rarely gets mentioned in CTV roundups because people think of it as "social," but on a TV screen it's the biggest streaming surface there is.

## What it actually costs

Realistic benchmarks, from the channels above: typical CTV CPMs run $25–45 on premium inventory, $18–30 mid-tier, and $12–22 on free ad-supported channels. Self-serve consoles skew lower — Amazon's self-serve streaming route is reported at roughly $8–15 CPM depending on targeting, well below premium DSP pricing.

Translate that into a test budget. A $500 campaign at a $25 CPM buys 20,000 full-screen impressions with sound on, non-skippable. That's not a rounding error — it's a genuine local market test. The comparison point that matters: [Q4 ad price surges](https://www.flexspot.lol/blog/q4-ad-price-surge-explained) hit the social and search auctions you're already buying, and [the political flood this October](https://www.flexspot.lol/blog/election-ad-flood-small-brands) crowded the exact inventory streaming buyers wanted. Streaming's auction dynamics are different from Meta's — broader inventory, less density — which is precisely why diversifying there has value right now.

The honest caveats: CPMs are the cheapest part of the bill if you don't have creative. Professional video production still starts in the low thousands. And the free services (Tubi, Pluto TV, the Roku Channel) run lower CPMs partly because the competition is lighter — check whether your actual customers watch them before you assume cheap equals good.

## The small-brand streaming playbook

**Start with the channel you can already feed.** If you have video — product clips, a testimonial, even a slideshow you export as MP4 — you have a streaming ad. Amazon's console offers creative services, and the AI-generation tools the platforms now ship can build a watchable 30-second spot from a website. Don't spend $5,000 on production to test $500 of media. That's backwards.

**Buy one market, not the country.** This is the structural advantage. Zip-code or DMA targeting on Roku and Google Ads means a Dubai restaurant or a Texas plumber buys only their households. Narrow targeting raises CPMs slightly — that's the documented trade — but it destroys waste. A national CPM of $15 you can't use is worse than a local CPM of $30 you can.

**Steal your social creative, then cut it down.** Your best-performing vertical video ad is your starting inventory. A 15-second cutdown with the logo in the first two seconds and a spoken call to action at the end beats a storyboard you're still revising in November. Streaming rewards the same discipline [the cheapest ad channels do](https://www.flexspot.lol/blog/cheapest-way-to-advertise-online-ranked): test the message small, then fund what works.

**Measure with foot-traffic logic, not click logic.** Nobody clicks a TV. Attribute like a billboard owner would: website visits in the targeted zip codes during flight dates, branded search lifts, promo codes spoken in the ad. Roku, Amazon, and Google all ship dashboards tying exposure to site visits and searches — use them, but keep your own baseline. Week-over-week comparisons inside a noisy October auction are meaningless; compare against your August numbers.

**Cap the test, don't cap the learning.** $500 across two weeks on one platform, one audience, one creative, one landing page. You'll know your real CPM, your completion rate (the percentage who watch the full spot — the metric linear TV never gave you), and whether visits moved. That's a go/no-go for a real budget, purchased for the price of a weekend social test.

## Why this matters before 2028

The crossover projection — streaming passing linear TV by 2028 — is a lagging indicator. The audience moved years ago; ad dollars follow audiences; buying tools follow ad dollars. Self-serve CTV is the last step in that chain, and it's here now.

None of this replaces search and social. A $500 streaming test sits next to, not instead of, the dollar-a-day experiments this blog has been running all along — [here's what a dollar a day actually buys](https://www.flexspot.lol/blog/dollar-a-day-advertising-what-it-buys). But it does change the ceiling. The attention marketplace is supposed to be an auction anyone can enter. For the biggest screen in the house, that finally seems to be true.
