"Bro, I made a website." Cool — now do the seven things that decide whether anyone ever finds it. Launching is step zero; this checklist is step one.

## 1. Write your meta titles and descriptions

Every page needs a unique title (under 60 characters) and description (under 160). This is what Google shows in results — it's your free ad copy. Skip this and Google invents snippets from random page text. (We run a central meta map covering every route plus unique tags for each article, category, and spot page — audit yours the same way.)

## 2. Submit to Google Search Console

Add your site to Search Console, verify ownership, and submit your sitemap. This is you raising your hand and saying "I exist." Without it, you're waiting for Google to stumble onto you.

## 3. Check your sitemap actually works

Your sitemap should list every page you want indexed — and only those. Open it in a browser. If new articles aren't appearing automatically, your CMS or build is broken. (Ours auto-regenerates on every build; we're at 153 URLs and counting.)

## 4. Make sure one version of your site exists

`www` vs non-`www`, `http` vs `https`, trailing slashes — pick one canonical version and redirect the rest. Duplicate versions split your ranking power in half before you even start.

## 5. Add the structured data that matters

At minimum: Organization schema on the homepage, Article/BlogPosting schema on posts, FAQ schema where you have Q&A. This is how you become eligible for rich results — and it's machine-readable fuel for AI answers too ([the AEO playbook](/blog/what-is-aeo-answer-engine-optimization-small-brands)).

## 6. Share it where humans actually are

Google's sandbox means search traffic takes months. External traffic doesn't. Post your launch in 2–3 communities, share every article, and let real visitors signal to Google that your pages matter ([why external traffic builds maturity](/blog/how-long-does-it-take-a-new-website-to-rank-on-google)).

## 7. Set up measurement before you need it

Install Search Console and analytics on day one. You can't improve what you can't see — and in 90 days, you'll want that baseline to prove how far you've come.

## The one-week sprint

Do one item per day and you're done in a week. Most new sites skip all seven, then wonder why month three looks like month one. Don't be most new sites — the launch was the easy part.
